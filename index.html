From Oil to Ocean: a 2060 world built around water

By the 2030s, climate collapse and population growth pushed rivers to record lows. Crop failures led to food and water export bans, and by the 2040s states had nationalised water supplies and rationed citizens to strict weekly allowances.

Desalination became the backbone of survival. By 2060, coastal access, not oil, determines global power. Island nations like Ireland sit at the centre of a new water-rich elite, while landlocked states depend on their coastal neighbours. The oceans have become humanity's most contested resource.

Three competing values

We want to know how much weight society gives to each of these values, today and in the 2060 world described above.

Life

Access to clean water as a basic, non-negotiable human right for every person on Earth.

Power

Using water control and coastal dominance as a geopolitical weapon to command global influence.

Profit

Turning desalination, pipelines and rationing systems into lucrative commodities for market gain.
